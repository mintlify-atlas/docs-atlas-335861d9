title: "Wikis, ideas, and docs in Leantime"

---

Leantime keeps your team's knowledge close to the work it relates to. Each project has its own wiki for structured documentation, an idea board for collecting and voting on ideas, a retrospectives canvas for reflecting as a team, and file storage for attachments. Comments and discussions are available on tasks, wiki pages, and project dashboards, so conversations stay in context.

## Wiki and docs

Each project gets a wiki — a hierarchical collection of pages where you can document processes, decisions, product specs, meeting notes, and anything else the team needs to reference.

### Creating pages

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the wiki">
    Click **Docs** or **Wiki** in the project sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a page">
    Click **New article**. Give the page a title.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your content">
    Use the rich text editor to write and format your page. The editor supports headings, bold, italic, bullet lists, numbered lists, tables, code blocks, and image embeds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. The page appears in the wiki tree on the left.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Organizing with hierarchy

Pages can be nested to create a hierarchy. To make a page a child of another, drag it in the wiki tree and drop it under the parent page. This lets you create sections like "Engineering > Backend > API design" without any extra setup.

<Tip>
  Use the wiki tree to mirror your project's structure — one section per major area of work. Team members can scan the tree to find relevant documentation without searching.
</Tip>

## Idea boards

Idea boards give the team a place to surface and evaluate ideas outside of the formal task workflow. Ideas can be created by anyone with project access, and team members can vote on them.

To open the idea board, click **Ideas** in the project sidebar. Click **Add idea** to post a new one. Others can upvote ideas they support. Sort ideas by votes to see what has the most traction, then promote the strongest ones into tasks when you're ready to act on them.

## Retrospectives

The retrospectives canvas is a structured board for team reflection at the end of a sprint or project phase. It organizes feedback into sections such as "what went well", "what didn't go well", and "what we want to change".

Access retrospectives from the **Strategy** section in the project sidebar. Add items to each section, discuss them as a team, and use the output to inform how you work in the next cycle.

## File storage

You can attach files to tasks, wiki pages, and projects. Files are stored either locally on your server or in an S3-compatible bucket, depending on how your administrator has configured Leantime.

To attach a file to a task, open the task and use the file upload area in the task detail panel. To attach a file to a wiki page, use the insert menu in the rich text editor.

Project-level files — like design assets or contract documents — can be uploaded from the **Files** section in the project sidebar.

## Screen and webcam recording

Leantime includes a built-in screen and webcam recorder. You can record a short video to explain a bug, walk through a design, or give asynchronous feedback, and attach the recording directly to a task or comment.

To start a recording, click the recording icon in the task toolbar or the comment input. Select whether to record your screen, your webcam, or both, then click **Start**. When you stop recording, the video is automatically attached to the task.

## Comments and discussions

Comments are available on tasks, wiki pages, and project dashboards. Click **Add comment** to start a thread. You can mention team members with `@username` to notify them directly.

Reactions let you respond quickly to a comment with an emoji without writing a reply.

<Note>
  All comments and discussions are project-scoped. They are visible to anyone with access to the project, but not to users outside it.
</Note>
